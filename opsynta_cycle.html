Questions you should ask

Straight answers.

How is this not just a retainer with a nicer name?

A retainer buys availability; the Cycle buys measurement with artifacts. Every month produces a numbered page — the seam's state, its movement, its cause — and every quarter produces a refreshed baseline. If a month produced no page, you wouldn't be paying for the thing you bought. The distinction is enforceable by you, which is the only kind of distinction worth paying for.

Why the 3-month minimum?

Because two data points is an anecdote and three is a trend line — the minimum is the cost of the line, not a lock-in trick. The whole product's value is watching the seam move over time; canceling inside the first quarter means paying for a measurement that never had time to become one. After the third month, you can stop at any month boundary, and the accrued credit converts at the quarter you leave.

Can we switch the seam we're measuring?

At quarter boundaries, cleanly — the new seam starts a fresh baseline with the next Deep Map credit. Mid-quarter switches are technically possible and almost always a mistake: they reset the trend and waste the two points you've paid for, so I'll advise against them unless the old seam genuinely died. The discipline of one seam is part of what you're buying.

What does the two-hour pulse actually do?

The same instrumentation as the diagnostic that opened the seam, compressed: pull the seam's operating numbers (adoption metrics, spend trend, or agent incidents), compare against the baseline and last month's point, and run one focused observation of the seam itself. Two hours sounds small because the method is instrumented, not exploratory — the diagnostic did the discovering; the pulse re-measures what was already mapped.

Can we buy the Cycle without having had a diagnostic?

Not through this page's buttons — and the honest reason is structural: the Cycle measures against a baseline, and without a delivered diagnostic there's no baseline, just two hours a month of unmoored observation. If you've had an equivalent instrumented diagnostic elsewhere, write to me directly and we'll talk about whether it converts. Everyone else's path in is a door on the grid — pick the one that matches your condition. The next onboarding opens Monday; after that, the following week or the four-seat waitlist.

What happens at the quarter's close, exactly?

The accrued $1,500 credit converts into a fresh Deep Map of the seam — five working days, the full method, for a net $1,000 (60% of the $2,500 list price already accrued). You choose whether to run it immediately or bank it for one quarter. The re-map is what keeps the monthly pulses honest: they measure movement, but the map is what tells you the movement is real and not instrumentation drift.

Why only four clients?

Arithmetic, honestly: four clients at roughly four founder-hours a month is sixteen hours — alongside the two-per-week diagnostic capacity, that's the whole calendar. The cap rises only with delegation, and delegation is exactly what would hollow the product out: the value of the pulse is that the same person who read the map re-reads the seam. The waitlist exists; the cap doesn't move for revenue.

When would you tell us to stop paying?

When the seam stops moving: the workflow's adoption has stabilized above target, or the bill's baseline holds for consecutive quarters, or the agent's drift indicators have been flat for two re-maps. Standing measurement on a stable seam is insurance against a risk that has retired — at that point the honest close is graduation, not renewal, and the twelve-page archive is what you keep. I'd rather be re-hired for the next seam than retained for a dead one.
