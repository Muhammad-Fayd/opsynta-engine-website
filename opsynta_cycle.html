Before you reserve a lane

Asked on every call.

Isn't this just a retainer?

Structurally yes, behaviorally no. It's a retainer with a fixed instrument (the pulse), a fixed artifact (the one-page report), and two calls — deliberately bounded. The point is that a map you paid for doesn't quietly go stale; the offer is specifically small enough that it stays a rung instead of growing into a tower.

Why four clients at launch?

The pulse is performed by the founder — the same person who runs every Deep Map, Pilot Autopsy, and Agent Autopsy. Depth is the product. Scaling the lane by diluting the instrument would kill the very thing the Cycle sells.

Who is this actually for?

Operations owners who have been through at least one diagnostic — usually the Deep Map or a Pilot Autopsy — and know the difference between a drawn map and a maintained one. If you haven't run an instrument, the Cycle is the wrong first purchase; start with the Deep Map.

How does the $500 quarterly credit work?

Every quarter you're in the lane, you accrue a $500 credit toward a fresh Deep Map. Three clean quarters is a $1,500 re-render for the price of one. The credit only exists if you use it — it's a standing incentive to re-instrument, not a loyalty treat.

What if the pulse finds nothing?

Then the one-page report says "nothing worth touching this month," and that becomes the baseline against which next month's drift is measured. The quiet months are product too — they're what makes the loud ones legible.
