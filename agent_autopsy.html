Before you flight-test

Asked on every call.

Is the battery generic or built for our agent?

Built for your agent. Day 1 is anatomy — the workflow, tools, model, and data that define what "failure" even means here. The battery then tests against this agent's own failure modes, never a template of someone else's happy path.

What does the verdict actually mean for our ship date?

Three verdicts, three outcomes: GO (ship, aware of the register), NO-GO (don't ship — and here's why, in ranked order), or CONDITIONAL (ship after the register items are fixed, and the protocol is how you prove it). The card costs a week; the wrong version of each costs a quarter.

Does "drift" mean you'll need production telemetry?

The drift read measures accuracy degradation across inputs and session length — we induce it in the battery rather than waiting for production to report it. The regression protocol you keep includes the instrumentation hooks your team should add for live, but the autopsy itself runs pre-ship.

How does the 70/30 partner lane work?

For agent-building firms that route a client's agent through the autopsy: 70% of the autopsy fee goes to the partner who holds the client contract, 30% to Opsynta — we're revenue for the builder, and the verdict gives the builder cover in both directions. Only if the partner wants Opsynta on the build itself does that become a separate agreement.

What access do you need to the agent?

The build itself — the workflow definition, tool contracts, model, and a judgment set to score against. We don't ship your code anywhere or connect to production. The battery runs in our environment against your agent's own conditions and stays entirely within the engagement.
