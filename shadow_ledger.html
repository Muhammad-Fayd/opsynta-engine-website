Before you open the ledger

Asked on every call.

We don't have "AI governance" — is this for us?

Especially for you. The ledger assumes no governance exists; it starts from the evidence that people are using tools anyway. The policy draft is built from what the sweep actually finds.

How do you find shadow AI without invasive monitoring?

We hold the line on that: no new monitoring software, no continuous surveillance, no agent on endpoints. Inventory is built from artifacts that already exist — access logs, purchase data, API traffic, interviews — then triangulated. The result tells you what's in use and where the seams are; it doesn't install a camera.

Are we too big or too small for this?

The ledger is scoped for teams up to about 150 people — big enough that shadow tooling is real, small enough that one instrumented sweep can actually see it. Above that size the volume stops being a week of work and we'll tell you so on the intake call.

Does the EU AI Act date apply to us even if we're not in the EU?

The risk-class grading uses the EU framework because it is the most complete published rubric. Where you operate sets which obligations bind you; the ledger grades against the framework and flags which classes are enforceable in your market. The map is useful either way — the risk classes name the physics of the exposure, not just the law.

What if the sweep finds nothing?

Then the verdict is "your perimeter held better than the average" — in writing. We don't manufacture inventory to justify the fee; the guarantee of the ledger is that it's honest about this company, not flattering to us.
